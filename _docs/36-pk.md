# 36 - Player killing

A character can be marked **PK active** for a while. If they die inside that
window it is a player kill, and it costs them levels, caps, everyone who knew
them, everyone they knew — and their head, left on the ground where they fell.

| | |
|---|---|
| `libs/sh_pk.lua` | the configs, the two-tier costs, who may mark |
| `libs/sv_pk.lua` | marking, collecting, and the forget list |
| `libs/cl_pk.lua` | the countdown on your own screen |
| `items/sh_playerhead.lua` | "&lt;name&gt;'s Head" |

| command | needs | what |
|---|---|---|
| `/pk <player> [why]` | `player.pk` | mark them, after a confirmation |
| `/pkoff <player>` | `player.pk` | take the mark off |
| `/pklist` | `player.pk` | who is marked, and what it would cost them |

Everything is on the **PK tab of the dev terminal**: the timers, all four cost
numbers, the notice range, and a list of who is marked with an UNMARK button
each.

## What came from Phoenix

Their `plugins/playerkilling`, with their configs and their split:

```
PK Level Reduction Above 50    2
PK Level Reduction Below 50    5
PK Money Cost Above 50      1000
PK Money Cost Below 50       500
```

50 is `ix.leveling.spike` here — the same number with the same meaning — so the
split lands where theirs does. Somebody at the ceiling has far more to lose in
absolute terms and loses fewer levels for it; somebody below it loses more
levels and fewer caps, because levels are what they have.

Also theirs and kept: the **confirmation** before marking somebody (`/pk` on the
wrong name costs them levels they cannot get back, and half the server shares a
first name), the countdown on the marked player's own screen at
`ScrW() * 0.25, ScrH() * 0.2`, and the **local** announcement — anybody within
800 units is told, so being marked is something the people around you saw
happen, not a server-wide broadcast.

That announcement is **red**, in Phoenix's own two-tone shape — the name in
`Color(255, 0, 0)` and the rest in `Color(255, 80, 80)`. `ChatPrint` is white
with no way not to be, which made it read like a server notice about a map
change, so the line goes over a net message and through `chat.AddText`.

Their server file is not in the scrape, as usual, so marking and collecting are
reconstructed from what the client and shared halves name.

### What was added

**A timer.** Phoenix's mark has none — it is on until they die or an admin turns
it off. `pkDuration` is what was asked for and it is the better rule: an
indefinite mark is one that gets forgotten and collected weeks later.
`pkMuggingDuration` is there for the mugging system that does not exist yet,
because the number belongs with the other PK numbers rather than in whatever
file eventually does the mugging.

**Recognition wiped both ways** — theirs does not do this.

**The head.**

### Becoming somebody else

Phoenix force a name change after a PK. Here it is a name **and** a description,
and it is not optional — it is the other half of what the recognition wipe
starts. Nobody knows them and they know nobody; a character who then walks back
into the same bar under the same name has not stopped being who they were, they
have just lost their address book.

**Forced means frozen.** A prompt that can be dismissed is a prompt somebody
dismisses. The panel has no close button and ignores Escape, but that is only
the polite half — the enforcement is that the server freezes them and will not
unfreeze until it has accepted both fields. The flag lives on the **character**,
so relogging brings it back rather than shedding it, and the prompt reappears
two seconds after the character loads.

The length rules are Helix's own, read from the same configs the character
creation screen uses so the two can never disagree about what a name is — and
they are shown on the panel, because being refused twice for a rule nobody
stated is how somebody ends up typing "aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa".

The prompt waits a second after death rather than appearing over the death
screen, which is the one moment nobody is looking at the game.

## Marking

`ix.pk.Mark(client, target, seconds, reason)` is **the one way in**, so the
mugging system has somewhere to call and nothing here has to be told about it.

The mark lives **on the character, not the player**, as an `os.time` stamp in
`pkUntil`. On the character so it survives a disconnect — otherwise `/pk` is
defeated by reconnecting, which is the first thing anybody would try. In
`os.time` because `CurTime` restarts at zero on every map load, and a mark saved
in it would either expire instantly or last for ever.

The HUD counts down a **netvar in `CurTime`** set alongside it. The character
data is the truth; the netvar exists because the client's `os.time` is its own
machine's clock and would be minutes out on anybody whose computer is wrong.

## Collecting

On death while marked, in this order:

**Levels.** `SetLevel(level - n)`, and **the XP comes down with it** —
`CheckLevel` walks upwards while the XP total is enough for the next level, so
dropping the level and leaving the XP alone means the very next kill puts it
straight back. The floor for the new level is what "you are level 12 again"
actually means.

**A respec.** Subtracting the points those levels were worth is not enough — the
points may already be *in* attributes, and taking them off the unspent pile
would leave somebody with five points of Strength bought with levels they no
longer have and a negative balance clamped to zero. A respec is the only
consistent state: attributes back to zero, points recomputed from the level they
are now. It does not count against their respecs and costs nothing; being killed
is not spending your free one.

`ix.leveling.Respec(character, bCounted)` was pulled out of the respec net
receive for this, so a chosen respec and an imposed one are the same code.

**Unspent points never blocked a respec**, incidentally: `CanRespec` asks three
things — level 50 or better, how many respecs are used, and whether they can
afford the next — and having points in hand is not one of them. Somebody who
saved their points and wants to move the ones they did spend is doing exactly
what a respec is for.

**Caps.** Not enough is not a debt — it is everything they had.

**Recognition, both ways.** Helix keeps it as a string of character ids on each
character (`rgn`, `",4,17,92,"`), so "this character knows nobody" is one field
to clear and "nobody knows this character" is their id removed from everybody
else's.

Everybody else includes people who are not here. An offline character's `rgn` is
a row in the database whose column holds a JSON blob of every piece of character
data there is, so instead the id goes on a **forget list** and the strip runs as
each character loads. Nobody is missed and nothing is rewritten by hand. It runs
every load rather than once — stripping an id that is not there does nothing, so
there is no state to keep about who has caught up. The list is pruned after a
month.

**The head**, at the death position rather than on the ragdoll — a ragdoll can
slide a long way in the moment after a death.

**And the mark is cleared.** Being PK'd once does not leave you standing there
still marked, so nobody can be collected twice off one `/pk`. Marking them again
is a second, deliberate `/pk`.

## The head

One item, `playerhead`, for every one of them. The name comes from `data.owner`
set on the instance, so each head is a distinct thing carrying a distinct name
without needing an item registered per character — which Helix cannot do anyway,
since items are registered once at load and characters are made afterwards.

**A permanent kill is the only head that names anybody.** Every other head is
cut off an anonymous body and carries `data.label` — an unmarked "NCR - Trooper
Head". A PK is the opposite case on purpose: the whole point of that head is
that it is somebody's.

**It is no longer dropped on death.** It used to spawn at the moment of death,
which gave the trophy to the *ground* rather than to anybody: it appeared
whether or not a soul was there to see it, and could be picked up by somebody
with nothing to do with the kill. A permanent kill now leaves a **body carrying
a name**, and the head has to be cut off it like every other head — three
seconds of standing over somebody you just killed, in a place where their
friends know where to look. It is the one corpse that shows a name, because a
trophy nobody can find is not a trophy.

Two rules protect it: **headpop never fires on a PK'd body**, so the shot that
earned the trophy cannot destroy it, and `corpsePKLife` (300s) keeps that body
around ten times longer than an ordinary one. See
[47-chat-tickets-and-gore.md](47-chat-tickets-and-gore.md).

`ix.pk.DropHead` is gone with the drop; `ix.corpse.TakeHead` is the only thing
that makes a head now, and `ix.corpse.headItem` is where the uniqueID lives.

The data goes in **at creation**, not set afterwards: `ix.item.Spawn` hands its
fifth argument to `ix.item.Instance`, which writes it into the row and copies it
onto the item before the callback runs. Setting it after would mean the entity on
the ground says "Head" until something made it look again.

It is worth **nothing** at a vendor. A head is evidence, a trophy or a message,
and a price would make it a farm — somebody would work out that killing marked
players pays better than looting.

### When there is no body to cut it from

A securitron has no head, a creature model may have no `Bip01 Head`, and a
model with no ragdoll data leaves no body at all. `ix.pk.Collect` asks
`ix.corpse.CanBehead(client.ixCorpseBody)` a frame after the death; where
that answers no, `ix.corpse.DropHead(where, name)` leaves the named head on
the floor at the death position instead. Everything with a human head still
has to be cut.

### Preparation for decapitation

`ix.pk.DropHead(character, position, angles)` is **the only place a head is ever
made**, and it takes a character and a position rather than a death. When
corpses can be decapitated — heads coming off, everything Phoenix has here that
this does not yet — that system calls this function and nothing in this file
changes. `ITEM.isPlayerHead` is the flag such a system would look for.

## Hooks

`OnPlayerMarkedForPK(admin, target, seconds, reason)`,
`OnPlayerUnmarkedForPK(admin, target)` and
`OnPlayerPK(victim, attacker, levelsLost, capsLost)` all fire, so a future
mugging, bounty or karma system can listen rather than being wired in here.

## A PK asks for a body, not just a name

The rename window asks for **height, weight and age** as well as the name and
description — the same three the creation screen asks for, in the same units,
with the same panels (`ixFOHeightEntry` is feet and inches, because nobody gives
their height in inches) and validated against the same constants in
`sh_biography.lua`.

Without them a PK produced a character who was word for word somebody else and
still 5ft 6in, 125lbs and nineteen years old, in the same body. A new person is
a new person.

## The suit comes off

Power armour is learned from a manual and forgotten at a permanent kill:
`Collect` clears `paTraining` and `ix.armor.StripPowerArmor` takes every
powered piece off the character before the respawn puts them back in it.
Salvaged frames (`isSalvagedPA`) stay on. See 20-armour.md.
